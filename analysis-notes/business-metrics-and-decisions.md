#  Analyst Decision Notes — Profit Leakage Analysis

##  Objective
The purpose of this analysis is to identify where and why profits are leaking despite healthy sales performance.  
Rather than focusing only on revenue, the analysis prioritizes margin health, discount behavior, and loss-making transactions to support better pricing and operational decisions.

---

##  Key Metrics Explained & Why They Matter

### 1️ Total Sales
**What it shows:**  
The overall revenue generated from all transactions.

**Why it matters:**  
Sales alone do not reflect business health. High sales can still coexist with poor profitability if costs and discounts are not controlled.

---

### 2️ Profit After Discount
**What it shows:**  
The actual profit generated after accounting for discounts applied to each transaction.

**Why it matters:**  
This metric reflects real business profitability. It helps identify whether discounting strategies are creating value or destroying margins.

---

### 3️ Overall Profit Margin (%)
**What it shows:**  
Weighted profit margin calculated as:
Total Profit After Discount ÷ Total Sales


**Why it matters:**  
This is the most reliable indicator of overall margin health.  
In this analysis, the overall margin remains positive (**0.97%**), but this hides deeper problems at the transaction level.

---

### 4️ Average Profit Margin (%)
**What it shows:**  
The simple average of profit margins across all transactions.

**Why it matters:**  
This exposes how many transactions are actually unprofitable.  
The average margin is **–9.57%**, indicating a large share of loss-making orders pulling margins down.

---

### 5️ Percentage of Loss-Making Transactions
**What it shows:**  
The proportion of orders where profit after discount is negative.

**Why it matters:**  
**32.4%** of transactions are loss-making, which is a major red flag.  
This suggests that discounting and pricing decisions are not aligned with cost structures.

---

### 6️ Discount Percentage
**What it shows:**  
The percentage discount applied relative to sales value.

**Why it matters:**  
Discounts are one of the biggest drivers of margin erosion.  
Higher average discounts correlate strongly with lower profit margins across categories.

---

##  Category-Level Insights

### Furniture
- Highest average discount (≈ **16.8%**)
- Lowest profit margins
- Indicates aggressive discounting without margin protection

**Decision implication:**  
Discount thresholds should be reviewed and capped.  
SKU-level pricing analysis is required.

---

### Office Supplies
- Strong sales volume
- Weak profitability

**Decision implication:**  
High volume does not guarantee profitability.  
Pricing efficiency and cost controls need reassessment.

---

### Technology
- Balanced discounting
- Highest and most stable margins

**Decision implication:**  
Technology pricing strategy is healthier and can serve as a benchmark for other categories.

---

##  Regional Insights

### North Asia
- Largest contributor to total profit
- Strong margin performance

### West & Africa
- Lowest profit contribution
- Higher proportion of loss-making transactions

**Decision implication:**  
Regional pricing and discount strategies should be localized instead of uniformly applied.

---

##  Key Business Risks Identified
- Over-discounting without ROI tracking
- High proportion of loss-making orders hidden behind strong sales
- Margin erosion concentrated in specific categories and regions
- Lack of transaction-level profitability visibility

---

##  Recommended Actions
- Introduce discount caps at category and SKU level
- Track loss-making transactions as a KPI, not just total sales
- Prioritize margin-focused KPIs over revenue-only metrics
- Perform deeper SKU-level profitability analysis
- Align regional pricing strategies with cost and demand patterns

---

##  Final Analyst Conclusion
Profit leakage is not caused by low sales but by inefficient discounting and pricing decisions.  
By shifting focus from revenue to transaction-level profitability, businesses can realistically improve margins by **3–5% annually** without increasing sales volume.
